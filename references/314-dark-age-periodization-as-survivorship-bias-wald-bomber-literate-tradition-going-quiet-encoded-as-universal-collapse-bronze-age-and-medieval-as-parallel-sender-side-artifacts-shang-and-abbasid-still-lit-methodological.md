## The claim

A "Dark Age" is, in the standard telling, a period when **civilization goes dark**. The framework's correction: in the canonical cases it is a period when **the specific literate record-producing institution that later Western historiography descends from goes dark** — and the silence of that one channel gets **encoded as the absence of civilization generally.** This is simultaneously:

- a **sender-side encoding** artifact (Shannon lens, cards 033/036/240): the record is *produced by* palace or monastic bureaucracies; when those institutions are disrupted, the channel stops transmitting. **Silence of the channel ≠ absence of the signal.**
- a **Wald-bomber / survivorship** artifact (card 064): "dark" marks exactly the region where the *recording instrument* was hit, not where activity stopped. Reading no-records as no-civilization is the survivorship error — inferring from the returning planes' bullet-hole map and missing the planes that never reported.
- **Euro-American (Dave's "west") vantage lock-in**: the historians who built the periodization worked *inside* the disrupted tradition, so they generalized their own region's discontinuity to a universal one. The narrative even encodes its own *resumption* — "civilization returns when Athens arrives" — which really means "the documentary tradition **we inherit** restarts," not that civilization restarted.

## The two parallel cases

**1. Bronze Age Collapse (~1200 BCE).** The eastern-Mediterranean literate palace bureaucracies stop: **Linear B tablets cease, Hittite archives cease, Egyptian records document the crisis.** Because that is the tradition feeding the later Greek writers who shaped Western historiography, the local blackout became "the collapse of civilization / a dark age until Athens." Meanwhile, on other instruments: **Shang China is at its height** in this exact window (Anyang/Yinxu, oracle-bone archives, ~1200 BCE), and the Central Asian bronze/tin world (BMAC substrate, the tin routes; cards 206, 313) shows **no equivalent collapse signature**. Civilization did not go dark globally; a *specific palace-bureaucracy model in one region* did.

**2. Medieval "Dark Ages" (~500–1000 CE).** Post-Roman Western Europe saw a real decline in administrative and monastic record-production in parts of the west. Simultaneously, on other channels: the **Abbasid House of Wisdom** in Baghdad, **Tang China** at cosmopolitan peak, and **Byzantium** running uninterrupted classical scholarship. The "Dark Ages" were dark *only* from a Western-European vantage mistaking its own regional discontinuity for a global one — which is why modern medievalists have largely **abandoned the term** (it traces to Petrarch's own sender-side agenda, valorizing antiquity over his present).

Same structure both times: **a genuine regional disruption in the tradition that later dominated Western historiography, over-generalized into a global narrative because the narrators sat inside that tradition and had thin visibility into everywhere else.**

## Why this matters

- **It is a flagship demonstration of the framework's core epistemology.** Card 240's "no view from nowhere / geography is the least-encoded channel": the *documentary* channel is heavily encoded by who was writing and whether their institution survived; **archaeology, other regions' records, material culture, and genetics are the second instruments that recover the Wald bomber the documents missed** (Shang oracle bones, BMAC archaeology, Reich's aDNA — cards 257/258/273). The multi-instrument method *is* the correction to the dark-age artifact.
- **Operational test (keeps it falsifiable, not just deconstruction):** to decide whether a named "dark age" is a *real* global collapse or a *channel artifact*, check the other instruments — did activity continue elsewhere (other literate regions, the archaeological/material record, genetics)? If yes, "dark" is a survivorship artifact of one silenced channel. If every independent instrument goes quiet together, the darkness is real. The framework predicts the *former* for the two canonical Western cases and does not claim it for all cases.
- **Precision — do not overcorrect.** The claim is **not** "nothing collapsed." The Bronze Age Collapse was a real, severe *regional* discontinuity (the Greek Dark Age after Mycenae — lost Linear B literacy, depopulation — genuinely happened *for Greece*); post-Roman western administrative decline was real *for the west*. The error is exclusively the **projection of a real regional blackout onto the globe.** Hold that line, or the card becomes its own over-correction.
