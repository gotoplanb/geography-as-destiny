## 1. Cunliffe's literal elevation gradient — a modest topographic backing (his word "gradient" carries no weight)

Cunliffe describes the **eastern (Mongolian) steppe as higher than the western (Pontic-Caspian) steppe**, with the **Altai** between, and suggests the drop "may have forced people west."

**Set aside the vocabulary point up front:** Cunliffe's use of the word "gradient" is **incidental prose, not a mechanism** — he is not deploying anything like the framework's thermodynamic production/sink/gradient apparatus (297/318 stand unchanged; he describes, he does not build the mechanism). It is *not* a meaningful "convergence on the framework's vocabulary," and nothing should be built on the word choice. What's worth keeping is the **substantive geographic fact**, not Cunliffe's phrasing.

**The substantive fact — a literal topographic gradient rhyming with the metaphor.** The framework's westward-flow story has run mostly on *pressure/resource* gradients (179, 185) — the metaphorical/thermodynamic "flow downhill." The elevation contrast adds a **literal** one: the Mongolian Plateau (~1000–1500 m) really does sit far above the Pontic-Caspian steppe (~0–200 m; the Caspian Depression is below sea level), so the dominant net-westward trend of steppe history also runs **literally downhill**. A satisfying literal echo of the metaphor — but keep the *gravity/downhill* reading **one modest forcing among several**, not a new prime driver: (a) the framework's main westward driver remains pressure-discharge toward rich, traversable farming targets (179/185); (b) major **eastward** projection is real and routine (Mongols into China; the billiard-ball reverse flows, 222/312). Elevation-as-slope supplies a slight net-westward *bias* consistent with the bidirectional model, not a one-way chute.

**Upgrade (2026-08-15, via card 324) — the elevation gradient matters more than "downhill," but through *productivity*, not slope.** Dave (pilot) supplied the real mechanism: higher terrain is colder (lapse rate) → colder air holds less water (Clausius–Clapeyron) → **less moisture → lower productivity → more marginal.** So the higher eastern steppe is not merely "uphill from the west"; it is **colder, drier, and lower-yield** — a stronger *pump source*, with the lower/warmer/wetter western steppe the richer *target*. That is a **productivity gradient, not a gravity gradient**, and it **compounds card 319's continentality** (the east is marginal on two independent axes: higher *variance* and lower *mean productivity*). This is the substantive reason the elevation contrast is worth noting — not the downhill reading (rightly kept modest above), and certainly not Cunliffe's incidental word "gradient." See **card 324** for the full mechanism.

## 2. Dave's Altai-junction radiation hypothesis — adjudicated

Dave's prior: populations hit the Altai junction and **radiate** east/west, without long *counterclockwise loops* that cross back past terrain they already traversed. As a geographic prior this is sound and already partly carded — the Altai is a **deep-time robust frontier / mixing-and-sorting node** (261, 265) and the steppe is a low-friction east-west transmission corridor with terrain bumpers and receiving basins (222). The framework already reads the Altai/Tien-Shan/Dzungarian region as a sorting hinge. Dave's worry was whether the **Finns** violate the "no weird loops" prior. They don't — but the resolution is more interesting than either Dave's or Beta's guess, and it turns on a corridor the framework hadn't yet named.

## 3. The Uralic/Finnic resolution (new territory) — 2025 aDNA: homeland in **Yakutia**, west along a **northern forest corridor**

Beta rightly flagged this as a separately-contested question (Uralic ≠ Indo-European/Yamnaya) and, to resolve the tension, guessed a northern forest population originating near the **Urals / middle Volga** that never went east. The targeted search resolves it decisively — and **corrects Beta's homeland guess while vindicating Dave's directional intuition**:

**Zeng et al. 2025 (*Nature*), "Ancient DNA reveals the prehistory of the Uralic and Yeniseian peoples"** (180 newly sequenced Siberians + >1,000 existing):
- **Homeland is northeastern Siberia — Yakutia (Lena River Basin), ~4,500 years ago**, *not* the Urals/Volga. The signature ancestry, **"Yakutia_LNBA,"** is a mix of North Eurasian hunter-gatherers + incoming East Asians.
- **It spread *westward*** from the Lena Basin **~4,000 ya into the Altai-Sayan region** and West Siberia, **associated with Seima-Turbino** metallurgy — reaching **Karelia ~3,500 ya** and the **East Baltic ~2,600 ya**.
- Modern gradient: **Nganasan ~100%, Finns ~10%, Estonians ~2%** Yakutia ancestry.

What this does to the two hypotheses:
- **Dave's far-east→west intuition is *vindicated*.** A population really did originate about as far east as Eurasia goes (the Lena) and move all the way west to the Baltic. The directional instinct was right.
- **The "counterclockwise loop feels weird" worry is *resolved* — via a corridor, not an exception.** It never looped back on the *southern steppe*. It ran along the **northern forest/taiga belt** (Yakutia → Altai-Sayan transit → West Siberia → Karelia/Baltic), a **separate latitudinal corridor** from the Indo-European steppe highway. No paradoxical steppe loop because it wasn't on the steppe track at all.
- **Beta was half-right and half-wrong (the correction-of-a-correction).** Right that Uralic is a *separate* population/language history from the Yamnaya/IE steppe material, and right that it's a *northern* phenomenon. **Wrong on the homeland** — deep Siberia (Yakutia), not the Urals/Volga. Beta's "separate northern population that never went east" is geographically inverted: the population *is* eastern in origin and moved *west*. This is exactly the CLAUDE.md discipline paying off: **both** the initial intuition **and** the plausible-sounding correction needed checking, and the aDNA landed on a third answer that neither had.
- **It hooks straight into existing framework cards.** The westward vector is carried by **Seima-Turbino** (~2200–1900 BCE), already in the archive (card 313's addendum; card 040); it transits the **Altai-Sayan robust frontier** (261) and the **Ancient-North-Eurasian / Baikal** zone (265). The framework already had the waypoints — it lacked the *corridor* and the *population*.

## 4. The reusable addition — the Two-Corridor Model of northern Eurasia

The payoff that generalizes beyond the Finns: **northern Eurasia carries (at least) two parallel east–west corridors, and the framework had only been tracking one.**

- **Southern STEPPE corridor** (grassland): Indo-European / Yamnaya-descended, horse-borne, the billiard-ball pressure-transmission highway (185/222). Net westward on the pressure gradient (179).
- **Northern FOREST / TAIGA corridor** (forest-steppe + boreal): Uralic / Yakutia_LNBA, Seima-Turbino-networked, moving west along the tree-line belt. Net westward as well — Lena to Baltic.

Both trend **net east→west** (consistent with a broad continental east-high/west-low tilt *and* the resource/pressure gradient), but they are **distinct populations, language families, and tracks**, running as parallel latitudinal bands with the **Altai-Sayan as a shared transit/sorting zone**. Dave's spatial model was right in spirit (radiate, don't loop) but was collapsing two corridors into one; separating them resolves the apparent Finnic anomaly and gives the framework a cleaner map: the steppe is not the *only* east-west channel, just the fast, high-friction-collapse one. The forest belt is the slower, parallel channel — and it is where the Uralic world came west.

- **New territory, cleanly resolved.** The framework had *zero* prior coverage of Uralic/Finnic origins or a northern forest corridor. This card opens it with a decisive 2025 source and ties it to existing waypoints (Seima-Turbino, Altai, ANE/Baikal).
- **A model evidence-strength catch.** A directional intuition (Dave) and a confident-sounding correction (Beta) both turned out incomplete; the aDNA vindicated the intuition's *direction* while relocating its *mechanism* to a corridor neither had named.
