# Lapita Founder Prestige Runs *Through* Accumulation, Not Around It — Bellwood's Founder-Rank Enhancement / Empty Islands as an Open Route to Rank That Closes When the Islands Fill / The Taiwan-Displacement Cause Fails

**Source:** Alpha source survey for issue #31, 2026-11-03. The issue came from a Beta seminar on Christina Thompson, *Sea People*, Part 3 (2026-06-16). Dave proposed that Lapita culture made exploration the *primary* path to prestige, displacing accumulation, because displacement from Taiwan left nothing to accumulate. Beta narrowed "uniquely valorized exploration" to "exploration displaced accumulation."
**Type:** verification. The proposed mechanism fails; a geographic reframing survives.
**Relevant to:** cards 278 (Pacific cascade; empty terrain beats proximate terrain), 289 (Out-of-Taiwan), 290 (Lapita pottery), 291 (tattoos as portable prestige), 292 (Proto-Oceanic); issue #31; issue #30

## What the evidence says

**Founding a settlement was a route *to* accumulated rank, not a substitute for it.** Peter Bellwood, "Hierarchy, Founder Ideology and Austronesian Expansion," in Fox & Sather, eds., *Origins, Ancestry and Alliance* (ANU Press, 1996), pp. 18–40, open access. Bellwood calls the mechanism **founder-rank enhancement**. A man from a junior line cannot inherit seniority at home, so he sails off and founds a settlement. He gains first claim on the best land and resources, and his descendants inherit the seniority he could not. "Separation… could have given founders opportunities for aggrandizement." Mounting a voyage also took canoes and crews, so successful founders were probably already fairly high-ranking. Bellwood rejects population pressure as "not an acceptable sole reason."

**Status in early Polynesia was genealogical, and later Polynesia was heavily accumulative.**
- Kirch & Green, *Hawaiki, Ancestral Polynesia* (CUP, 2001): the chiefly title *ariki* is reconstructed for the earliest period, based on seniority of descent within house societies.
- Kirch, *The Lapita Peoples* (1997), "Houses with Ancestors": ranked house societies running prestige-goods exchange.
- Kirch, *How Chiefs Became Kings* (2010): once the islands filled, Hawai'i became an archaic state with divine kingship.
- Goldman, *Ancient Polynesian Society* (1970), confirmed only secondhand: status ran along several paths — seniority, war and economic standing.

**Taiwan was not a society without wealth to accumulate.** Hung et al. 2007, *PNAS* 104: Neolithic Taiwanese nephrite circulated as portable valuables and was exported. That cuts directly against the premise that displacement left nothing to accumulate.

**Founder ideology is older and wider than Lapita.** Vischer, ed., *Precedence: Social Differentiation in the Austronesian World* (ANU Press, 2009), including James J. Fox: status by origin and founding order is widespread across Austronesian societies, including island Southeast Asian ones that never colonized by sea. It was inherited, not produced by displacement.

**Lapita obsidian weakly supports a relational rather than hoarding economy, but the debate is open.** Constantine et al. 2015 find obsidian not tied to individual burials. Torrence 2004 notes stemmed obsidian tools predate Lapita.

## Resolution of the issue's sub-questions

- **A (did exploration replace accumulation as the main path to prestige?): likely no as worded (~75%).** Founder prestige is real and well supported, but it worked through land, seniority and later monuments and tribute. It sat alongside the other paths rather than displacing them.
- **B (was displacement from Taiwan the cause?): likely no (~70%).** Founder ideology was shared across Austronesia, Taiwan had jade wealth, and there are strong rival explanations:
  - Bellwood's founder effect.
  - An ideal-free-distribution push (Kennett, Anderson & Winterhalder 2006): settled islands become less attractive as they fill.
  - Multi-cause models (Anderson & O'Connor 2008).
  - Weakest point: whether Proto-Austronesian society was ranked remains unresolved.
- Issue #30 independently undercuts the upstream premise: card 289's "closed westward channel" trigger is not supported either.

## What survives — and it is the framework's own logic

The issue's proposal ran the causation from scarcity: there was nothing to accumulate, so prestige redirected to exploration. The evidence runs it from **opportunity**.

**A supply of empty islands, which is geography, made "found a new senior line" an unusually open route to rank for junior lines.** Every voyage that found land converted a junior son into a founding ancestor. The route stayed open exactly as long as the frontier did. When the islands filled, the same prestige system turned inward into competition for land, labour and tribute, and Hawai'i and Tonga stratified into kingship.

This is card 278's "empty terrain beats proximate terrain" applied to the prestige economy rather than to population movement. The prediction it carries: **voyaging prestige should be highest during open-frontier phases and decline into accumulative stratification as each archipelago fills.** That is testable against the settlement chronology. It also supplies the Wald-survivorship structure: the founder lines we see in the genealogies are the voyages that found land.

**What this card does not claim:** that Lapita was unique in valorizing voyaging. Norse, Phoenician and Greek comparisons remain as Beta framed them. The distinctive variable is the *length* of the open frontier — roughly three millennia of island discovery — not a different set of cultural values.

## Calibration

1. Do not add a "substrate-redirected prestige economy" mechanism on the strength of Lapita. The issue's own rule was to require both A and B before naming a new mechanism, and both likely fail.
2. The opportunity reframing is a framework reading of Bellwood, not Bellwood's own claim. He states founder-rank enhancement; this card adds that the empty-island supply set its payoff and its closure.
3. Card 291 (tattoos as portable prestige) is unaffected. It concerns the medium of prestige display, not whether prestige came from voyaging or from accumulation.
