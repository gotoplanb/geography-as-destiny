# Pacific Migration Models Share the One-Way Frame — Express Train / Entangled Bank / Slow Boat All Refuted by the DNA's Bidirectional Sloshing

**Source:** Beta seminar discussion, 2026-06-16, during Dave's listening of Christina Thompson, *Sea People: The Puzzle of Polynesia* (William Collins, 2019)
**Type:** connection
**Relevant to:** Foreword (historiographical positioning, probabilistic-history commitment), Chapter 1 (Reich cluster, pressure-wave model), general methodology

## Content

Pacific archaeology has been engaged for decades in a debate among three competing models for Polynesian origins. Thompson lays them out in *Sea People*:

- **Express train** (Jared Diamond, "Express Train to Polynesia," *Nature* 336, 1988; drawing on Peter Bellwood's earlier Out-of-Taiwan synthesis) — fast, directed expansion from Taiwan eastward through the Pacific, with each new island settled in turn and earlier populations either skipped over or replaced
- **Entangled bank** (John Edward Terrell, *Prehistory in the Pacific Islands*, 1986) — complex and non-linear, emphasizing the persistent contribution of indigenous Melanesian populations and the messy interactions among multiple source populations along the expansion route
- **Slow boat** (Stephen Oppenheimer, *Eden in the East*, 1998; Martin Richards et al. mtDNA work) — long-distance migration from Island Southeast Asia or Wallacea, gradual and incremental over millennia rather than rapid and directed

The debate has been productive. Each model has caught something the others missed, and the literature has matured by working through their differences. Of the three, the Slow Boat is closest to the framework's spirit — it allows the most gradualism and complexity.

But the framework's methodological critique is sharper than picking among the three. *All three models share the same underlying frame.* Population A starts at origin; population A moves toward destination; the movement is the story; the destination is the end state. The three models differ on speed, route, and degree of admixture along the way, but they all share the basic structure of *one-way directed migration*. Express Train is fast and clean; Entangled Bank is messy but still origin-to-destination; Slow Boat is slow but still A-to-B.

The ancient DNA (Reich and collaborators, 2018, and the subsequent Lapita-period and post-Lapita aDNA literature) reveals something different in kind. Not a wave moving east. Not even a complicated wave with eddies. Continuous bidirectional flow along the corridors the outrigger canoe (card 278) made traversable, with the direction and intensity of flow shifting over time with climate, resources, demographic pressure, and the available technology at each moment. The genetic signal at any given Pacific island reflects *cumulative input from many sources over many centuries*, not the founding signature of a single arrival event.

The Lapita people (cards 290, 291, 292) are not a founding population that arrived and stayed. They are a moment in a continuous mixing process that was already happening before them and continued after them. The expansion that produced recognizable Polynesian populations a thousand-plus years later was the cumulative output of that continuous process, not the radiating consequence of a single founding event.

This is the framework's pressure-wave model (card 273) applied to Pacific prehistory. Express Train, Entangled Bank, and Slow Boat are three variants of a linear-migration encoding the actual mechanism does not match. The framework rejects the shared frame and proposes the pressure-wave model as the substitute: multi-directional flow shifting under pressure-differential dynamics across the substrate the outrigger made traversable, with the genetic record as the honest cumulative trace of the flow.

Beta's compression of the methodological move, worth preserving verbatim: *The DNA is the honest record. The migration models are the sender-side encoding of prehistory — clean narratives imposed on a messy bidirectional reality because clean narratives are easier to teach and publish.*

## Why this matters

This refines the framework's historiographical positioning in three ways.

1. **The linear-encoding error extends from 19th-century anthropology into contemporary scholarship.** Card 287 documents Tregear's Aryan Māori thesis (1885) as the 19th-century version of the error. This card shows that the linear-migration encoding has carried forward into late 20th- and early 21st-century academic debate. Diamond's Express Train, Terrell's Entangled Bank, and Oppenheimer's Slow Boat are all post-1980 products. The encoding error is not specific to Victorian anthropology — it persists wherever scholarship retains the one-way migration frame, regardless of its sophistication or its rejection of earlier specific errors.

2. **The framework's pressure-wave model operates as a diagnostic tool, not just a descriptive one.** When the framework sees Express Train vs. Entangled Bank vs. Slow Boat being debated as competing alternatives, the diagnostic move is to reject the shared frame all three operate within. The same diagnostic applies to other contemporary debates that share the linear-migration assumption:
   - **The Bantu expansion debate.** Origin in Cameroon vs. multiple-wave variants — all retain the one-way frame. The pressure-wave model predicts continuous bidirectional mixing across the corridors farming made traversable.
   - **The Indo-European spread debate.** Kurgan/Yamnaya vs. Anatolian vs. various intermediate positions — Reich's data has begun to push the field toward more complex pictures, but the popular and even much scholarly framing still defaults to one-way migration of named populations.
   - **The peopling of the Americas debate.** Clovis-first vs. pre-Clovis vs. coastal-migration variants — all retain origin-to-destination structure. The pressure-wave model predicts bidirectional and multi-source mixing along the Beringian and Pacific-coastal corridors.

   In each case the framework predicts that the shared frame is the problem, not the choice among variants. Worth carrying the diagnostic explicitly.

3. **Distinguishes the framework's probabilistic-history commitment from the framework-aligned-looking alternatives.** Of the three Pacific migration models, the Slow Boat is closest to the framework in spirit because it allows the most gradualism and complexity. But even the framework-aligned-looking option is still operating within the one-way migration frame. The framework's probabilistic-bidirectional-mixing commitment is more radical than the best of the existing alternatives. *Closer to the framework* is not the same as *of the framework*. The framework's methodological commitments are stricter than picking the best of the available linear-migration options, and the discipline of holding that distinction is what prevents the framework from being absorbed into "yet another nuanced migration model."
