## Beta's Framework Synthesis

**The wagon-as-mobile-home articulation:**

> The mobile home analogy is precise. Before the wagon you're tethered to water sources you can reach on foot or horseback carrying what you can carry. The wagon doesn't just increase carrying capacity — it makes your entire household mobile. The tent, the food stores, the children, the elderly, the accumulated material wealth of a pastoral family — all of it moves with the herd.

**The previously-unuseful-land friction-collapse articulation:**

> The open steppe between reliable water sources was essentially uninhabitable for sustained occupation before the wagon. You could raid through it. You could seasonally graze it. You couldn't live in it year-round with a family and full household.
>
> The wagon collapses that friction. Suddenly the entire steppe is habitable because you carry your water supply infrastructure with you in the form of containers and you range further between sources. The land area available for sustained pastoral occupation expands dramatically overnight in historical terms.

**The Maykop-Yamnaya transmission confirmation:**

> Reich confirming the Maykop as the likely transmission vector for wheel technology to the Yamnaya is exactly what the framework predicted from the Caucasus friction sieve logic. High value technology crossing the mountain barrier from the Anatolian-Mesopotamian south to the steppe north. The Maykop culture sitting on the northern slope of the Caucasus as the relay station.

**The technology-origin vs technology-scaling distinction:**

> The wheel didn't originate on the steppe. The steppe population acquired it through the friction zone contact network and then scaled its use in ways the originating culture never imagined because the steppe terrain made the wagon's potential so much larger than anywhere else.
>
> Same pattern as the horse. The initial domestication probably happened at the terrain boundary. The explosive scaling happened on the open steppe where the resource base made it possible.
>
> The Maykop gave the Yamnaya the wheel. The steppe gave the wheel its maximum expression.
