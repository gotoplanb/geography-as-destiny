## The prediction, in three parts (as Ch4 states it)
1. **Gradient + timing:** earliest sustained settlement clusters in the *middle-river energy-moderated "Goldilocks" zone* (not the erosive headwaters, not initially the delta); dense urban centers arise *later*, downstream on the lower floodplain / delta.
2. **Temporal succession:** first movers take the Goldilocks zone; later phases concentrate downstream.
3. **Asymmetry:** a *hard upstream boundary* (sharp cutoff) and a *softer, gradual downstream boundary*.

## Per-civilization verdicts (sourced)
**Nile — asymmetry CONFIRMS; gradient/timing PARTIAL→cuts-against.** Hard upstream boundary is textbook: the **First Cataract at Aswan / Elephantine** ("everything north was Egypt, everything south Nubia"; the fortified "door of the south"); soft, shifting swamp-to-meander delta edge (Pennington, *Landscape change in the Nile Delta 4th millennium BC*). But the *earliest farming* was **downstream** — Faiyum A (~5500–4400 BCE) and Merimde (~4800 BCE) on the delta fringe — while the earliest *state* was upstream in **Upper Egypt** (Badarian/Naqada, Hierakonpolis; Stevenson 2016, *J. Archaeol. Research* 24). Because the Nile flows north, "Upper Egypt" is *upstream*; the state trajectory ran upstream→downstream (to Memphis at the delta apex, ~3100 BCE). The "Goldilocks energy zone" physics **doesn't transfer** to single-channel flood-basin irrigation — there is no erosive headwaters *within* Egypt, so "not the headwaters" is vacuously true. Caveat: early delta sites are buried under alluvium, so "Upper Egypt was first" is partly a preservation/visibility artifact.

**Mesopotamia — asymmetry CONFIRMS; urban-later-downstream CONFIRMS; middle-river origin CUTS AGAINST.** Earliest farming was in the **rain-fed uplands / hilly flanks** — Braidwood's **Jarmo** (Zagros piedmont, ~7000 BCE), the PPNA/PPNB Levant, Göbekli Tepe — *off the river entirely* (multi-regional "protracted" model; ScienceDirect 2017). Cities came **later and downstream** in the southern alluvium (Ubaid/Eridu ~5400 BCE → Uruk ~4000 BCE; Adams, *Heartland of Cities*). Controlling variable is the **~200 mm rainfall isohyet** (dry-farming vs. irrigation; Jursa), *not* river-energy gradient; the southern cities sat in the *worst* natural zone (near-zero rainfall) and were viable only via **engineered irrigation** — the opposite of a "natural Goldilocks optimum." The Samarran middle-Tigris/Diyala fringe (Choga Mami ~6000 BCE) is a real intermediate zone, but as an *irrigation-technology stepping-stone*, not a first-settled energy-moderated valley. Hard upstream (rainfall isohyet at the piedmont) + soft, silting, contested Gulf coastline downstream.

**Indus — all three PARTIAL / cuts-against.** Earliest farming is **Mehrgarh** (~7000 BCE) on the **Kacchi plain at the foot of the Bolan Pass — piedmont/uplands WEST of the Indus**, not the trunk river (a direct parallel to Mesopotamia's hilly-flanks origin). Mature cities (Harappa on the Ravi; Mohenjo-daro on the middle-lower Indus) come ~4,000 years later on the alluvial plain. **Two hard disconfirmations:** (a) origin is upland/piedmont, not middle-river; (b) **no downstream delta metropolis** — the delta was unstable and largely *avoided*, and the densest settlement (>1,000 sites: Kalibangan, Banawali, Rakhigarhi) lies on the **now-dry Ghaggar-Hakra paleochannel**, i.e., an *abandoned* river, not a live trunk gradient (Giosan et al. 2012 *PNAS*; Singh et al. 2017 *Nat. Commun.* — "the departure of the river, not its arrival, triggered urban growth"). Asymmetry is real but not the predicted shape: upstream (avulsive Himalayan rivers) hard, downstream/delta *also* hard/avoided, soft edges running east/south across interfluves. The single-river-gradient model is fundamentally incomplete here.

**Yellow River — all three CONFIRM (cleanest case).** Earliest sustained farming clusters in the **middle loess valley** (Peiligang ~7000–5000 BCE, Yangshao ~5000–3000 BCE) — the "cradle of Chinese civilization"; the earliest state (Erlitou ~1900 BCE) is still middle-valley; dense **Shang** urban centers (Zhengzhou, Anyang) expand *later* onto the flood-prone **North China Plain** downstream. Hard arid upstream cutoff (Ordos / Majiayao / Tibetan margin) + soft, dynamic, flood-graded downstream. **Two caveats:** (1) the single-cradle model is outdated — the **multi-regional** consensus (Su Bingqi / K.C. Chang; the independent lower-Yangtze rice center, Shangshan→Liangzhu) means the Yellow River is not China's *sole* cradle; (2) the middle-valley advantage rests partly on **rain-fed loess/millet soil**, not purely river-energy moderation.

## What the test actually shows (honest synthesis)
**Confirmed, robustly:**
- **The upstream boundary is hard** in all four cases (Aswan cataract; rainfall isohyet; Ordos aridity; avulsive Himalayan front). This is the strongest, cleanest result.
- **Settlement avoids the violent headwaters and the earliest states/cities arise later on the alluvial plain, downstream of the origin point.** Broadly borne out (Nile, Mesopotamia, Yellow River; site-selection scale everywhere — levees, elevated well-drained ground).

**Cuts against / needs correction:**
- **"Earliest settlement in a middle-river energy-moderated Goldilocks zone" is wrong for two of the four primary civilizations.** In the Fertile Crescent (Mesopotamia) *and* the Indus, farming **originated in the rain-fed uplands / hilly flanks** (Jarmo; Mehrgarh), off the trunk river, and descended to the alluvial plain *later*, via irrigation. Only the **Yellow River** is a clean middle-valley-origin confirm.
- **The controlling variable is frequently rainfall + soil, not river-energy gradient.** Where the model gets the *direction* right, the actual cause is often the dry-farming/irrigation rainfall threshold (Mesopotamia) or loess soil quality (Yellow River) — the "energy dissipation curve" is a partial proxy, not the mechanism.
- **The "soft downstream boundary" and the "delta-city culmination" are Nile/Mesopotamia-specific, not universal.** The Indus delta was unstable and avoided (hard, not soft; no Ur/Memphis equivalent); densest settlement sat on a *dry* paleochannel.
- **Line 30's claim — "the archaeological record confirms it across *every* major river civilization on Earth" — is a clear overclaim and must be walked back.**

## Net verdict
**PARTIAL confirmation.** The prediction's *structural core* — settlement avoids violent headwaters, clusters in a stable zone, and dense urbanism arises later on the plain, with a hard upstream boundary — holds well and is a genuine, sourced confirmation. Its *signature specific claims* — a "middle-river Goldilocks energy zone" as the origin of settlement, a universal soft-downstream boundary, and a delta-city culmination — fail or need major qualification in two or three of the four cases, chiefly because (a) farming often originated in the **rain-fed uplands, not the river valley**, and (b) the controlling variable is often **rainfall/soil, not river energy**. Reported straight, this *strengthens* the chapter: it converts an untested (and partly overclaimed) assertion into a tested, nuanced, sourced claim — exactly the book's log-the-misses discipline.

**The deeper alignment (Dave's reframe):** the miss is *toward* the framework, not against it. A single dimension (river-energy gradient) being only partially predictive is exactly what **substrate-conjunction** (interlude) and the **probabilistic** frame (Ch1 — geography sets the *distribution*, not the outcome) predict: no one variable is sufficient alone. Ch4's own **five-variable model** (climate, geometry, cycle, outlet, adjacency) already committed to this. Had river energy *alone* predicted settlement everywhere, one dimension would have been doing all the work — which substrate-conjunction says cannot happen. The rain-fed uplands did not break the model; they supplied the term (climate/water/soil) a one-dimensional proxy had dropped. So the partial result is the framework's multi-variable/probabilistic commitment confirmed from the other side — the reduction failed, the framework held.
