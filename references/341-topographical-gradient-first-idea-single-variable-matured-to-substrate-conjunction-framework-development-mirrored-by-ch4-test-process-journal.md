## The intellectual history (Dave's, first-person)
The **topographical / river gradient** was one of the framework's **founding ideas** — and a good one: a *single* variable (elevation energy dissipated along a river's course) that carried a lot of explanatory weight, predicting where settlement should concentrate, why deltas are dense, why the headwaters are empty. It had high explanatory power for one term. That is exactly why it was a first idea: single-variable models that explain a lot are the ones that hook you.

Then came the reading — many books, many Beta seminars, many years — and the single variable acquired company. The framework accreted **more variables**: climate/rainfall, linear geometry, a predictable flood cycle, a connected outlet, favorable adjacency (Ch4's **five-variable model**), and above them the organizing concepts that say *no single variable is sufficient* — **substrate-conjunction** (interlude: several conditions coinciding produce outcomes none would alone) and the **probabilistic frame** (Ch1: geography sets the *distribution* of outcomes, not the outcome). The gradient did not get discarded; it got **demoted from sole cause to one term among several** — still real, still doing work, no longer carrying the whole load.

## The recapitulation (why this matters beyond autobiography)
Card 340's holdout test of the settlement-gradient model **retraced this arc from the outside, in the evidence.** The test's naïve, single-dimension version — score a river's energy curve, read off where people should be — is the *first idea* in operational form. It came back only **partially predictive**: the asymmetry and the cities-later-on-the-plain pattern held, but the "earliest settlement originated in a middle-river Goldilocks zone" claim failed for the Fertile Crescent and the Indus, where farming began in the rain-fed uplands and the controlling variable was rainfall/soil, not river energy. In other words, **the evidence forced exactly the single-variable → conjunction move that Dave's own thinking had already made over twenty years.** The author matured from gradient to conjunction by reading; the archaeology confirms the maturation was the right direction.

This is the framework's development and its validation pointing the same way:
- **As development:** a concrete, honest instance of the framework maturing — a founding single-variable insight enriched, not overturned, by accumulated reading. (The opposite of the great-man "lone flash of genius" story: the framework is an accretion, and its best early idea was a *reduction* that later work corrected.)
- **As validation:** the mixed holdout is *more* persuasive than the clean one (Niya, 054/055) precisely because it caught the framework's own early over-reduction and the correction runs *toward* the framework's stated commitments (substrate-conjunction, probabilism), not away from them.

## The general principle (worth stating)
**Single-variable models that explain a lot are seductive first ideas and reductive final ones.** A variable with high explanatory power is where a framework should *start* — it is the signal that a real mechanism is present — but a mature framework expects any one variable to be *partially* predictive, because outcomes are conjunctions drawn from a distribution. So a founding variable being only partly predictive on rigorous test is not a failure of the idea; it is the sign the framework has grown up around it. Watch for the inverse failure mode too (the framework's intellectual-honesty commitment, Ch11): the pull to keep crediting the founding variable with more than its share because it was first and it was yours.
