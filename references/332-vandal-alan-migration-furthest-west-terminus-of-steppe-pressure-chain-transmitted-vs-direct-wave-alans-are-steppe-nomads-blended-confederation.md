## Dave's corrected observation (verified)
The migration Cunliffe describes without naming is the **Vandal-Alan** movement. Verified: on **31 Dec 406** a blended confederation — **Hasding and Siling Vandals + Alans + Suebi**, with smaller **Sarmatian, Gepid, and Herulian** contingents — crossed the Rhine near Mainz, "progressively displaced westward by **Hunnic raids and intertribal conflicts**." They moved through **Gaul → Iberia → across Gibraltar into North Africa (429)**, founded a kingdom that lasted into the 6th century, and **sacked Rome from the sea in 455**. This is the **furthest-west/southwest reach of a steppe-originated pressure wave since the Yamnaya** — and it went *further* than Yamnaya, across Gibraltar into Africa.

## Framework move 1 — the transmitted-wave refinement (extends 222)
Dave's load-bearing point, held precisely: the **originating steppe group (the Huns) did not travel to the Atlantic or Africa.** The **pressure propagated** west through a chain of intermediate populations. This is card **222's billiard-ball / occluded-front mechanism at its western terminus** — and, as Dave notes, a *cleaner* illustration than a direct-travel claim would be: it is a **wave transmitting *energy* through a *medium*** without the medium (or the originator) traveling the full distance. The Huns are the disturbance; the displaced Germanic-and-steppe peoples are the medium; North Africa is where the energy finally dissipates on the far shore — and then rebounds to sack Rome (455). A downstream consequence of pressure originating far to the east, several generations and multiple population-hops removed — 222's "China sneezes, Rome catches a cold" carried to its extreme (the cold reaches North Africa and comes back by sea).

## Framework move 2 — two modes of steppe pressure reaching west: *direct* vs. *transmitted*
This case sharpens a distinction the framework had left implicit:
- **Direct-wave (Yamnaya, ~3300–2600 BCE):** the steppe people *themselves* expand west — demographic replacement, the movers travel the distance (cards 171/185/269/303).
- **Transmitted-wave (Migration Period, ~5th c. CE):** the steppe originators *stay east*; the pressure **propagates through intermediaries** to reach the far west. The Vandal-Alan case is the **flagship of the transmitted mode reaching furthest** — and, notably, it reaches *further* (into Africa) than the direct Yamnaya wave did, precisely *because* propagation through a medium can outrun the originator. Same steppe-pressure engine, two delivery modes; naming them keeps the mechanism honest.

## The correction (verify-don't-punt) — a steppe-nomad component *did* reach North Africa
Dave's framing that "the furthest-west extent was reached by a **Germanic** population, not the steppe-originating group" is *slightly* too strong, and the correction is more interesting than the original claim: the confederation that reached North Africa was **blended Germanic + Iranian-steppe**, and it **included genuine steppe nomads — the Alans** (Iranian steppe nomads), plus **Sarmatians** (also Iranian steppe nomads). The Alans rode the *entire* distance to North Africa; the kingdom was styled **"rex Vandalorum et Alanorum"** (King of the Vandals *and Alans*). So it is **not** a case of a Germanic proxy standing in for absent steppe peoples — a steppe-nomad component *did* traverse the full distance to Africa, embedded in a blended confederation. **Dave's core point still holds** — the *Huns / the originating pressure* did not travel the full distance; the pressure *propagated* — but the refinement is sharper: the wave was carried west by a **blended Germanic-steppe confederation** in which steppe nomads (Alans) reached the Atlantic and Africa, not by a purely Germanic vehicle.

## Framework move 3 — the mover was a catchall blended confederation (316/175)
This is why Cunliffe "didn't name the specific group": the "Vandal" migration was a **fluid, multi-ethnic coalition** (two Vandal groups + Alans + Suebi + Sarmatians + Gepids + Herulians), not a pure ethnicity. That is the same **catchall-confederation** point the framework applies to the Goths (316) and in the naming-impulse catalog (175, where "Goth" is a coalition label, not an ethnos). The Migration Period movers were **blended coalitions absorbing diverse peoples en route** — Germanic *and* steppe-Iranian — exactly the robust-frontier plurality the sedentary sources flatten into single tribal names. The Vandal-Alan confederation is a new instance for the 175 catalog: a blended *Germanic-plus-steppe* mover.

## Calibration / open items
1. **The causal joints are debated — hold the chain as structural/probabilistic, not a hard genealogy.** (a) The **Xiongnu→Hun** identification is a longstanding but contested hypothesis (per 222, hold as structural, not proven descent). (b) The **Hun→Vandal(406)** link is *"Hunnic raids AND intertribal conflicts"* — Hunnic pressure is *a* driver, not the sole cause; the *cleanest* Hunnic-driven case remains the **Goths (376 → Adrianople 378, card 316)**, while the 406 Vandal crossing is more multi-causal (Hunnic pressure + intertribal dynamics + the Roman frontier crisis of 405–406). Don't overstate a clean Hun→Vandal trigger.
2. **"Furthest west since Yamnaya" is a strong framing — scope it.** It's the furthest *steppe-originated pressure-wave* reach in the framework's post-Yamnaya sweep; state it as such, not as an absolute record, and pair it with the Alan nuance (a steppe *component* did travel, so "no steppe people reached Africa" is wrong).
3. **This is an *extension*, not a new mechanism.** The engine is 222's billiard-ball / occluded-front; this card adds the western terminus, the direct-vs-transmitted modes, and the blended-confederation instance. Cross-reference; don't re-derive 222.
4. **Verify Cunliffe's actual Ch. 8 account** (which groups he names, what causal chain he draws) before prose; the Vandal/Alan identification and the transmitted-wave framing are the framework's, built on Dave's corrected reading.
