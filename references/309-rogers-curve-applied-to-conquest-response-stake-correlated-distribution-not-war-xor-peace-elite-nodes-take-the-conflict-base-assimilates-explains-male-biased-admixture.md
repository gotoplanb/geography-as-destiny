# The Rogers Curve Applied to Conquest *Response* — Displacement Is a Stake-Correlated Distribution, Not War-XOR-Peace / Elite Nodes Take the Conflict (Replacement), the Base Assimilates ("New Boss, Same as the Old Boss") / This *Explains* the Male-Biased Admixture Signature

**Type:** framework refinement — the diffusion lens extended from innovation *adoption* to conquest *response*; reconciles and completes card 308's collaboration cut

## Content

Dave's refinement (explicitly framed *not* as 100% and *not* as provable — a probability distribution): the Yamnaya arrival in Europe was not peaceful, but "violent replacement vs. peaceful collaboration" is the **wrong binary.** The right model is a **distribution of responses correlating with existing social position / stake**, read through the Rogers segmentation:

- **Innovators / early adopters — the resource-holding, status-invested, risk-taking — concentrate the conflict, on *both* sides.** The Yamnaya elite arriving to *claim* territory and the resident European elite *holding* territory both have skin in the game and the most to lose. Conflict, resistance, and destruction concentrate at this node. This is where genuine population replacement shows up.
- **Late majority / laggards — those without significant resource holdings or investment in the status hierarchy — assimilate.** They have little reason to resist and more reason to accommodate. Their daily labor, subsistence pattern, and social relationships change little whether the chief speaks a Yamnaya-derived language or the old one. **"New boss, same as the old boss."** This is broad population continuity and gradual absorption.

**The Genghis Khan parallel.** Same structure: elite resistance and elite destruction get documented and remembered; the ordinary population's largely continuous daily existence under new administration attracts little documentary attention because it wasn't dramatic enough to record. The Yamnaya came in with a *similar Neolithic package* — so for the base, it genuinely was a change of management, not of life.

The net signature this predicts: **replacement concentrated at certain social and geographic nodes (elites, contested resource zones), with continuity and gradual absorption elsewhere** — exactly the mixed, regionally-variable, replacement-*dominant*-but-not-total pattern the aDNA shows (card 308, Calibration 2).

## Why this matters

**1. It extends the diffusion lens from adoption to conquest *response* — same segmentation, new application.** Rogers segments a population by *response to an innovation*; Dave applies the identical segmentation to *response to displacement*, with the sorting variable being **stake / resource-position / risk-tolerance** rather than adoption-propensity. This is a genuine extension of the framework's third lens (and of card 307's laggard-mechanism work): conquest outcomes are a *distribution*, not an event, and the distribution is predictable from who had what at stake. It also operationalizes the standing methodological key (percentages = Rogers-curve positions) on a non-adoption phenomenon.

**2. It reconciles and *completes* card 308's collaboration cut — the disciplined middle, not a retreat.** Card 308 correctly cut the naive "culturally aligned peoples collaborated peacefully" story as doing too much work. Card 309 supplies what belongs in its place: **neither flat collaboration nor flat replacement, but a stake-stratified distribution** — replacement at elite nodes + assimilation at the base. This is *more* correct than either pole and matches the evidence better than both. The 308 cut removed a false mechanism; the 309 model installs the true one. (Note the contrast with the earlier over-validation of issue #33: here the refinement is disciplined precisely because Dave hedged it as a distribution he can't prove, not an asserted proportion.)

**3. It turns the male-biased admixture signature from a *rebuttal* into a *prediction*.** In card 308, male-biased admixture (cards 270/281) was used to reject flat peer-collaboration ("asymmetric power, not balanced admixture"). The stratified model does better: it **predicts that exact signature.** Elite-node conflict = the incoming male elite displaces the resident male elite and takes local women; the base assimilates. That *is* male-biased admixture — replacement at the male-elite node, continuity at the base. So the stratified conquest-response distribution is not contradicted by the genomics; it is the *mechanism that produces* the male-biased signature. This deepens 270/281 from "asymmetric power" (generic) to a specific structure: **elite-node replacement + base assimilation.**

**4. Wald-bomber operates on the conquest record — anti-great-man discipline.** The elite node is *dramatic, monumentalized, documented, and archaeologically legible* (destroyed strongholds, elite burials, martial goods). The continuous base is *undramatic and largely unrecorded*. Reading conquest as "total war" or "total replacement" off the elite-weighted record is survivorship bias in the documentary/archaeological instrument (cards 048, 099, 228, 285): the returning bombers are the elite conflicts loud enough to leave a mark; the quiet continuity of the base is the engine-hit data that didn't get recorded. The bulk pattern (base continuity) is systematically under-weighted precisely because it wasn't dramatic — the same anti-great-man correction the framework runs everywhere.

## Calibration

1. **Dave's own hedge is load-bearing — this is a probability distribution, not a demonstrated mechanism.** Dave explicitly said he *can't prove it* and is not claiming 100%. Hold card 309 as a **strong structural hypothesis** that is *consistent with* the genomic (male-biased, regionally variable) and documentary (elite-weighted) signatures — not as established fact. Its strength is that it predicts the observed signatures from a single stake-correlated distribution; that is corroboration, not proof.

2. **The Rogers segment is a proxy for stake/risk here, not literal tech-adoption behavior.** "Innovators/early adopters get into conflict" works because those segments *correlate with* resource-holding, status-investment, and risk-tolerance — the variables that actually drive the response. Keep the mapping explicit: the sorting axis is **what people had at stake in the existing hierarchy**, which loosely tracks Rogers segmentation. Don't rigidly equate "early tech adopter" with "resource-holding elite"; state the correlation, not an identity.

3. **Male-biased admixture → elite-node replacement stays mechanistic, not lurid.** Per cards 270/281: state the inference as a structural/statistical claim about asymmetric power at the elite node, explicitly separated from moral narration, and don't over-specify the lived experience the genome doesn't record ("the genome records the outcome, not the experience").

4. **Regional variation is the distribution doing its work, not noise.** Some zones show near-total replacement, others substantial continuity. Under this model that variation is *expected* — it reflects local differences in social structure, resource concentration, and how much elite-node conflict there was to concentrate. Consistent with the probabilistic frame (cards 010 / 304 / 307).

5. **The Genghis Khan parallel is illustrative of the documentary-weighting mechanism, not a claim of identical process.** Use it for the "elite destruction remembered, base continuity unrecorded" point; don't over-map specifics across very different eras, records, and terrains.
