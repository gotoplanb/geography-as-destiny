## Calibration — 2026-06-04

The Constantinople reading recorded in this exchange — Constantine's 330 CE relocation as self-inflicted friction collapse, "Constantine dug a canal toward the economic gradient and accidentally drained the reservoir" — was revised on closer examination of the tetrarchy and the century-long eastward operational drift that preceded Constantine.

Diocletian's tetrarchy (293 CE) had already split imperial administration across four capitals — Nicomedia, Sirmium, Mediolanum, Trier — none of them Rome. The eastern tax base, the Persian frontier, and the Danube defensive line had been pulling the operational center eastward for a century before Constantine was born. Constantine in 330 CE formalized a structural drift that was already in motion. He did not initiate it. He built the permanent city at the point where the river was already flowing.

The framework therefore removes Constantine from the Appendix A Section 6 "self-inflicted friction collapse" case list. A single-actor catastrophic-canal reading of Constantine would concede the great-man ground the framework otherwise rejects. The structural reading both fits the evidence better and sharpens the boundary between Section 6 (self-inflicted friction collapse, reserved for actors who genuinely optimize away their own structural advantages) and Section 7 (the anti-hero pattern, reserved for actors who dig catastrophic canals against substrate flow).

The cognitive-pattern observation that this card was originally filed to document — human generates the analytical move, AI elaborates and provides vocabulary — remains intact. The leap recorded in the original exchange was a real leap; the leap's substantive content required revision on closer reading. Both can be true. This is the framework operating its own self-correction discipline: when closer examination reveals that what looked like an individual-actor catastrophic decision was actually a formalization of pre-existing structural drift, the case gets reclassified. The discipline matters more than any specific case the discipline reclassifies away from.

The "potentially Abbasid Damascus → Baghdad" and "Mongol capital shifts eastward" candidates listed in the parked Appendix A material above remain on the Section 6 list pending closer examination. Each is subject to the same discipline applied here: if structural drift can be shown to have predated the formal capital relocation, the case fails the category test.
