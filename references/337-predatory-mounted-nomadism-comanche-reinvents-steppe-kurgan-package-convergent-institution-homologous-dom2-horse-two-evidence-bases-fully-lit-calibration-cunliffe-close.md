## The convergence — what actually matches
Two mounted-pastoralist societies, ~4,000 years and an ocean apart, converge on the **same institutional package**:
- **Open grassland terrain + horse mobility + adjacent sedentary wealth** → a predatory-and-trading economy in which **status and power accrue from raiding and trading the prestige goods of sedentary neighbors.**
- **Steppe:** mounted predatory nomads whose elites accumulated imported prestige goods — the archetypal rich case is the **Scythian kurgans** stuffed with **Greek-made goldwork** and Near-Eastern imports; the older, non-mounted end of the same burial tradition includes the **Maykop** kurgan (~3700–3000 BCE), rich with imports reflecting contact with Near-Eastern civilization.
- **American Southwest:** the **Comanche** (Hämäläinen, *The Comanche Empire*, 2008) — after horses spread north following the **1680 Pueblo Revolt**, they built by the 18th century a raiding-and-trading empire extracting horses, Spanish metal/guns/textiles, and captives from sedentary Spanish, Pueblo, and Mexican settlements, redistributing them through a trade network they dominated.

Same thermodynamic setup → same institutional draw. This is the framework's core claim in its strongest form: a **terrain-driven, predictable pattern**, not a culturally contingent one-off.

## Sharpening #1 — convergent INSTITUTION, *homologous* HORSE (correct "completely unconnected")
The tempting phrasing — "two completely unconnected cases, zero cultural transmission" — is right about the **institution** and wrong about the **enabling animal**, and the correction makes the framework point *stronger*, not weaker.

The Comanche horse is not an independent domestication. It descends, via Spain and Iberia and the whole Old World, from the **same DOM2 lineage** — the single Western-Eurasian-steppe domestication (~2000 BCE, Sintashta horizon) that **all** modern domestic horses trace to (**Librado et al. 2021**, *Nature*). The very animal that powered steppe predatory nomadism in the first place completed a ~4,000-year global loop and was **re-delivered to the American grassland** by the Spanish.

So the two cases are **convergent at the level of the social-military-economic package** and **homologous at the level of the animal.** The institution was reinvented; the horse was the same lineage arriving again. This is the "cosmically-precise" flavor of card 269: same terrain-logic + literally the same domesticated animal's descendants → the same institutional outcome, twice. Do **not** claim independent domestication or a fully-severed connection.

## Sharpening #2 — the parallel is the predatory-nomad-prestige STRUCTURE, not the burial FORM (color-is-a-smell)
The prestige economy **expressed differently** at each end. The steppe expression the evidence hands us is **monumental burial** — the kurgan, with grave goods (metalwork, gold, imports). The Comanche produced **no kurgans**; their prestige economy ran through **living horse-wealth, raiding reputation, captive-taking, and trade-network dominance**, not monumental interment. The match is the **mobility-enabled predatory accumulation of prestige from sedentary neighbors** — the *structure* — not the burial *form*. Forcing a grave-goods parallel would be color covering for a weaker point; the structural parallel is the real and sufficient one.

## Sharpening #3 — it is the *ridden* (mounted-cavalry) nomad the Comanche match, i.e. the Iron-Age steppe type
Predatory *mounted* nomadism — raiding on horseback — is the **ridden-horse** development, which on the steppe matures in the **Iron Age** (Scythians the archetype), *after* the Bronze Age wagon-pastoralist Yamnaya and *after* the Sintashta chariot. The Comanche are mounted raiders, so their cleanest steppe analogue is the **ridden-cavalry predatory nomad** (Scythian-type), not the wagon-borne Yamnaya to whom cards 195/281 draw the genomic/federation parallel. This is consistent with the framework's chariot/cavalry nuance (325): the enduring steppe predatory pattern belongs to the **ridden horse**, not the wagon or the chariot. "Kurgan" is a burial-form marker spanning the whole pastoralist→mounted-nomad continuum (Gimbutas's usage); the *predatory-mounted* version is its later, ridden phase.

## The load-bearing epistemics — two INDEPENDENT evidence bases (not one source pattern-matched)
The strength of this as evidence is precise, and it is **not** "both of us arrived at it independently" (the framework has drawn steppe↔Comanche parallels for many cards — 195, 281, 112 — so *within the framework* it was sought, not stumbled on). The real independence is at the **source / evidence-base** level:
- **Cunliffe** builds the kurgan-predatory-nomadism-prestige concept **entirely from Eurasian steppe archaeology**, with zero reference to or knowledge of the Comanche.
- **Hämäläinen** builds the Comanche empire **entirely from American Southwest ethnohistory**, with no reference to the steppe.

Neither reads into the other; there is **no shared source and no diffusion of the institution.** So the structural match cannot be an artifact of pattern-projection onto a single source. An **independent scholarly tradition, working from an independent body of evidence, corroborates a parallel the framework had already built** — which is exactly what raises confidence that the framework is describing a general mechanism rather than a pattern visible only where it went looking.

## Methodological payoff — the Comanche is the fully-lit calibration case for the mute kurgan (per 316)
Card 316 named the *fully-documented calibration case* as an epistemic instrument (the richly-recorded Goths calibrating inference about the mute Sea Peoples). This is the same move on the steppe: the kurgan leaves **mute grave goods** — we infer the predatory-nomad-prestige economy from the metalwork in the ground. The Comanche leave **a dense ethnohistorical record** — we can *watch* the living raiding-and-trading prestige economy operate. The recent, fully-lit case lets us **read the mute ancient one**: the Comanche show us the behavior the kurgan can only imply.

## Three lenses
- **Geographic determinism:** open grassland + adjacent sedentary wealth sets the distribution; the same terrain draws the same institution on two continents.
- **Friction collapse:** the horse is the mobility/velocity input (the ridden horse crossing the war-use threshold) that makes predatory nomadism viable — the same enabling collapse, delivered by the same animal lineage, twice.
- **Diffusion:** prestige goods diffuse from sedentary neighbors into the nomad prestige economy (302, 327); horse adoption itself runs a fast Rogers curve on a maximally-compatible population (284).

## Why this matters
- **Closes the Cunliffe arc on a clean confirmation:** an independent Old-World archaeological tradition corroborates the framework's flagship New-World case — general mechanism, not local pattern.
- **Sharpens rather than flatters:** the homologous-horse and institution-not-burial corrections keep the claim precise and falsifiable.
- **Demonstrates 316's calibration-case method** on a second pairing (Comanche↔kurgan, after Goths↔Sea Peoples).
