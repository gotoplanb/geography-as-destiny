## The apparent problem
Appendix A §10's falsification clause lists as a falsifier: "Expansion-phase empires systematically adopting extraction policies." The Neo-Assyrian Empire (c. 911–609 BCE) looks like exactly that. From Tiglath-Pileser III's reforms (745 BCE) through its 8th–7th-century peak, Assyria ran an *extraction* economy — tribute, systematic mass deportation, booty, corvée/forced labor, wealth concentration in the core, terror codified in the royal annals — during its most aggressive *expansion*, not merely in a late contraction phase. On the phase-pairing alone, that reads as a counter-case.

## The resolution — it fails the falsification test on the decisive count
The load-bearing claim of the cycle is not the phase-pairing; it is the *causal core*: extraction policy produces short-term revenue and long-term **substrate erosion**. Assyria confirms that core at maximum amplitude:
- **The economy never became self-sustaining.** It depended on continued conquest and tribute inflow; it did not convert its conquests into an organic, market-bearing provincial economy that could carry the empire once expansion stopped. (Assyria *did* build real provincial administration, roads, and a standing army — it was not pure plunder — but the fiscal base was extraction-dominant, not general taxation on organic growth.)
- **The collapse was total and permanent, not survivable.** When the core faltered after Ashurbanipal (d. c. 631 BCE), Assyria did not fragment-and-persist the way Rome did into Byzantium or the Chinese dynastic order did into successor dynasties. Nineveh was destroyed in 612 BCE by the Medo-Babylonian coalition; the last resistance at Harran fell by 609; the Assyrian heartland was never restored to imperial status. The most powerful empire the world had yet seen vanished within a generation of its peak.

That totality-of-collapse **is** the substrate-erosion prediction realized — not a violation of it. Dave's framing is exact: Assyria never met the stability threshold the enduring empires reached, *because* it ran extraction from the start.

## The sharpening — the phase-pairing is a survivorship property (Wald)
Assyria is therefore the case that shows *why* the "expansion = markets" pairing holds among the empires we study: **extracting during expansion is self-limiting.** An empire that runs extraction from the outset pays the substrate-erosion cost early and does not survive to join the set of long-enduring empires whose policy histories are legible to us. So the observed regularity — enduring empires enabled markets during their expansion — is *partly a selection effect*: the Assyrias, the empires that extracted during expansion, are the bombers that did not return (Appendix C's Wald apparatus). We don't see them in the "enduring empire policy history" sample because extraction-during-expansion kept them out of it.

This splits §10's claim cleanly into two tiers:
1. **Falsifiable causal core (load-bearing):** extraction → substrate erosion. *Falsified* only by extraction systematically producing long-term substrate *growth*.
2. **Derived tendency (survivorship-shaped):** expansion↔markets, contraction↔extraction. This holds *among survivors* and is explained by tier 1 plus selection. Apparent exceptions to the pairing (Assyria) do not falsify tier 1; they illustrate the selection that produces the pairing.

## Macro/micro (keeps §10 honest)
The proximate triggers of the fall — the civil war following Ashurbanipal's death, the Nabopolassar–Cyaxares coalition, and a probable mid-7th-century drought (Schneider & Adalı 2014) — are the random-walk steps. The extraction-hollowed substrate is the macro condition that determined the *character* of the collapse: total and irreversible rather than survivable. A more organically-grounded empire absorbs the same shocks and fragments-but-persists; Assyria had no such cushion to fall back on.

## Proposed edits to Appendix A §10 (done in this session)
- Add a "**Sharpening case — the Neo-Assyrian Empire**" beat resolving the apparent falsifier.
- Rewrite "What would falsify it" to center the causal core: extraction producing long-term substrate *growth* is the real falsifier; extraction-during-expansion followed by collapse (the Assyrian pattern) confirms the core and reveals the phase-pairing as a survivorship property.

## Calibration / open items
1. **Assyria built genuine administration** — don't caricature it as pure plunder; the point is the fiscal *base* was extraction-dominant and non-self-sustaining, and the collapse was uniquely total.
2. **Multiple collapse causes** — the substrate-erosion reading is the framework's lens on why the collapse was *total*, not a claim that extraction was the sole proximate cause; hold the megadrought/civil-war/coalition triggers as the micro-level steps.
3. **"Partly a selection effect"** — keep the survivorship claim hedged; the phase-pairing is a real tendency reinforced by selection, not a pure artifact.
4. **Endurance is relative** — Assyria lasted ~300 years as a state; the compressed, total window is *peak-to-annihilation* (the great conquests to 612 BCE) versus the fragment-and-persist trajectories of Rome/China.
