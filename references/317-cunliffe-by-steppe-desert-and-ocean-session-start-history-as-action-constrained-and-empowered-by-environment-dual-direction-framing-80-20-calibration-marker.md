## The book

Cunliffe, *By Steppe, Desert, and Ocean* (OUP, 2015) — ~9000 BCE to the 14th-c. CE Mongol expansion; the full Eurasian sweep from farming origins through the **steppe corridor** connecting Europe, the Near East, and China. The framework-relevant stance (per reviewers): Cunliffe treats **steppe Asia as a center, not a periphery**; declines to view nomads only through sedentary-literate eyes (avoiding the "barbarian" framing bias — cf. cards 175/269); and **does not privilege textual over archaeological evidence**, treating them as equally valid — which connects directly to the framework's Wald-bomber / sender-side-encoding methodology (cards 240, 316).

## The Ch. 1 observation — the definition of history

Cunliffe opens with a definition close in spirit to: **history is human action constrained *and* empowered by environment.** (Paraphrase — Dave's rendering from listening; the exact Ch. 1 wording is not yet verified against the text. A targeted web pass did not surface the precise sentence; confirm against the physical book before any load-bearing quotation.)

**Why the "constrained *and* empowered" pairing is more precise than a simple environmental-determinism claim:** it captures **both directions** of the relationship rather than treating environment as purely limiting. This is closer to the dissertation's own **friction-collapse** concept than a static Diamond-style determinism would be — environment does not just constrain what is impossible; it **empowers** what becomes newly possible when terrain and technology conditions align. Documented dissertation cases that fit the dual framing:

- The horse doesn't just constrain pre-domestication mobility — it **empowers** Yamnaya expansion once domesticated (cards 171, 185, 269).
- The Gansu/Hexi Corridor doesn't just constrain nomadic invasion routes — it **empowers** Chinese defensive concentration at a single chokepoint.
- The Bosphorus doesn't just constrain naval access — it **empowers** whoever holds it with a defensible capital position for centuries (card 225).

Note also Cunliffe's own pressure-wave phrasing in the opening: the steppe/desert/ecotone environment producing "restlessness and occasional **tsunamis of aggressive movement**" — his term for the same phenomenon the framework carries as the steppe pressure-wave / billiard-ball apparatus (cards 222, 316). His definition and the dissertation's thesis are close kin: **environment sets the probability distribution of historical outcomes** (the book's own core claim) sits comfortably beside Cunliffe's "constrained and empowered."

## Calibration — the 80/20 overclaim, flagged and corrected

An earlier draft of this observation proposed a specific quantitative claim (**environment ≈ 80% of historical variance, individual action ≈ 20%, framed as regression coefficients**). **This numeric framing overclaims relative to the dissertation's actual evidentiary practice** and is not adopted. Several documented cases — the **An Lushan Rebellion** most sharply — show individual/contingent factors *selecting the catastrophic draw from an available distribution*, rather than environment alone determining the outcome. The defensible position, and the one the framework holds:

> **Environment sets the distribution of likely outcomes and dominates the variance in the most rigorously tested cases — without claiming a fixed universal ratio.**

This keeps the observation inside the **probabilistic-language editorial principle** (geography shapes distributions, not outcomes; contingency selects the specific draw). Any prose that cites Cunliffe's definition should carry the distribution framing, not a numeric coefficient.
