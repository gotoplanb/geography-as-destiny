# Out-of-Taiwan as Taiwan-Resident Redirection Under Mainland Pressure — Agency on the Taiwan Side, Not Mainland Stepping-Stone

**Source:** Beta seminar discussion, 2026-06-16, during Dave's listening of Christina Thompson, *Sea People: The Puzzle of Polynesia* (William Collins, 2019), Part 3
**Type:** connection (refinement of card 278)
**Relevant to:** Chapter 1 (cascade derivation), Foreword (anti-eugenics positioning), general thesis

## Content

Card 278's existing cascade derivation reads, in its compressed form: *"The risk-takers who missed out on the Goldilocks zone kept going south until they eventually reached the water. They jumped over to Taiwan, which is close, and from there, the Austro-Sino expansion occurred."* The popular framing of Out-of-Taiwan reads similarly — mainland Chinese populations push south, hit ocean, jump to Taiwan, launch into the Pacific. Taiwan as stepping stone.

This gets the agency wrong, and the framework's anti-eugenic commitment makes the difference load-bearing.

The linguistic evidence is decisive on where the actual Austronesian homeland is. The Formosan languages of Taiwan are the deepest branches of the Austronesian family. Malayo-Polynesian — which contains every Austronesian language outside Taiwan, including every Polynesian language — is *one branch* of Austronesian, sitting alongside the Formosan branches in the family tree. The whole Out-of-Taiwan expansion is one twig on a tree whose roots are firmly in Taiwan. Robert Blust's reconstruction of the family puts Proto-Austronesian on Taiwan by ~3500–2500 BCE; Malayo-Polynesian, the launch-branch, separates from the Formosan tree only after that. The Dabenkeng/Tapenkeng archaeological complex (c. 4000–2500 BCE) shows long-resident Neolithic populations on Taiwan with established subsistence and material culture well before any seaward expansion.

So Out-of-Taiwan was launched by people who had been on Taiwan for at least a millennium, plausibly more. They were not mainland Chinese who arrived, paused briefly, and continued. They were Taiwan-resident populations whose ancestors had reached Taiwan generations earlier and whose connection back to the mainland was a *channel they had been using*, not a recent arrival path.

The framework's question is: why did they launch *then*? The framework's hypothesis: the westward channel closed under mainland pressure. The Yangtze region rose into the densest agricultural civilization in East Asia. The pressure differential that had previously allowed Taiwan-mainland exchange compressed. Whatever cultural, mercantile, or political projection Taiwan-resident populations had been able to maintain westward became less viable as the mainland side filled up and consolidated. Faced with a closed westward channel, those populations developed and intensified the seaward channel instead. The outrigger canoe, the systematic island-prospection practice, the navigation tradition that Beta synthesized in card 278 — these emerged as the answer to *"where can we go now that we can no longer go west."*

The cascade story doesn't change in its broad shape. Risk-takers follow gradients. Friction collapses via the outrigger. The Pacific gets settled in the order the friction map predicts. But the launch site has Taiwan-side agency, not mainland-side momentum carrying through. Taiwan-resident populations redirected because they had to. The expansion was the answer to a closed channel, not the continuation of an open one.

## Why this matters

This refines the framework's Pacific cascade derivation (card 278) in three load-bearing ways.

1. **Anti-eugenic framing sharpened.** Card 278's existing language can be read — uncharitably but legibly — as *the strong Chinese followed the gradient and conquered the Pacific via Taiwan as their launching pad*. That reading is wrong in spirit but the framing leaves room for it. The Taiwan-resident-redirect framing closes that room. The agency is on the Taiwan side. The trigger is environmental — pressure from the mainland blocked one channel and another channel opened. The expansion is *substrate-driven redirection*, not radiating superiority. This is the cleaner anti-eugenic move and the one the framework should prefer.

2. **Sharper application of the framework's thermodynamic gradient logic.** Card 276 names the framework's deepest substrate as thermodynamic — pressure differentials and substrate constraints drive outcomes. The Taiwan-resident-redirect framing applies that logic precisely: pressure differentials do not move populations toward "manifest destiny" outcomes. They redirect populations whose previous channel of activity gets blocked. The Pacific cascade is what happens when Taiwan populations can no longer project westward and find the eastward channel wide open. The mechanism is redirection-under-pressure, not radiating-strength.

3. **Generalizes as a framework lens.** Many expansions in the framework's archive may be similarly readable as *redirect-under-pressure* rather than *radiating-strength*. The Comanche horse-empire formation after mainland pressure on their original Shoshone-substrate territory (cards 100, 113, 284). The Atlantic crossings as European-coast redirection after Ottoman closure of overland eastward channels. The Russian eastward expansion across Siberia as response to Western European demographic-and-military pressure. Each is worth re-reading through the redirect-under-pressure lens to see whether the agency-attribution shifts. The framework predicts: many will. The "strong-people-radiating-outward" reading is the eugenic default, and the framework's discipline is to reach for the redirect-under-pressure reading first and let the radiating reading earn its way back in only when the evidence forces it.

## Open evidence-strength question

The Yangtze-rise-as-specific-trigger remains contested and is the subject of issue #30. The Taiwan-resident-launch portion of this card is solidly supported by linguistic and archaeological evidence. The pressure-from-mainland-as-trigger portion is framework hypothesis pending the issue's resolution. Whether the Yangtze rise was itself driven by upstream Yamnaya energy injection (Dave's original framing) or by internal Chinese substrate dynamics is a further open question downstream of this one.
