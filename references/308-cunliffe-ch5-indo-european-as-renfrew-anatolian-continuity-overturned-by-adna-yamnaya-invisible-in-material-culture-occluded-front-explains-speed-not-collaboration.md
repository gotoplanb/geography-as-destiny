## Content

Cunliffe closes Ch. 5 by **alluding to the steppe intrusion but never naming the Yamnaya** and never describing an incoming steppe/Iranian-plateau-adjacent population. He frames the period as **continuity**: the same western/northward-moving European populations, adopting the Neolithic package at different rates, with **Indo-European language arriving as part of that package and spreading by creolization.**

This is not idiosyncratic — it is **Colin Renfrew's Anatolian hypothesis** (*Archaeology and Language*, 1987): Indo-European spreads *with farming* out of Anatolia, carried by the Neolithic dispersal, not by a later steppe migration. Cunliffe (writing 2008, pre-aDNA) reads the material record through that lens. Dave's own note — "this would be hard to pull out from the archaeological record" — is exactly right, and it is the crux.

**Why Cunliffe couldn't see the Yamnaya: the instrument, not the reality.** Material culture *cannot cleanly distinguish population movement from adoption.* Pottery styles, burial practices, and new domesticates can spread either by people moving or by locals adopting. From material culture alone, a large population intrusion and an indigenous adoption of new practices can look nearly identical. So the Yamnaya are a **Wald-bomber case (cards 167, 258, 285): invisible in the instrument Cunliffe had, unmistakable in the instrument he lacked.** His not naming them is a *limitation of material culture as a detector*, not evidence of continuity. The aDNA revolution (Anthony, Reich) supplied the missing instrument and settled it: the steppe/Yamnaya expansion was **real, large, and involved substantial population replacement** across many European regions — the framework's Kurgan/steppe position (card 189), confirmed. Cunliffe's continuity reading was the *reasonable pre-aDNA conclusion* and *wrong about the mechanism*.

**Dave's occluded-front extension — and where it splits into a right half and an over-reaching half.** Dave applies card 303's occluded-front / legibility-inheritance: Europe's populations were *already* mobile, pioneering, and adopting pieces of the Neolithic package (the slow front, having done the reconnaissance); the Yamnaya then poured in carrying their own package via the long steppe loop (the fast front), and — Dave adds — were *culturally aligned* in a shared "pioneering spirit," implying substantial **collaboration** between the resident Europeans and the Yamnaya.

- **Right half (keep): the occluded front explains the SPEED.** The legible, already-pioneered landscape (card 303) is why the steppe expansion could move fast on arrival — the reconnaissance friction was pre-paid. This survives.
- **Over-reaching half (drop, per Beta): cultural-alignment collaboration as an explanatory mechanism.** Beta's pushback is correct and this card endorses it. The aDNA shows **demographic overwhelm — substantial replacement/absorption of the resident farming populations within a few generations in many regions — not peaceful co-adoption between culturally aligned peers.** And the framework's own cards sharpen *why the collaboration story is unlikely*: the admixture is **male-biased** (cards 270, 281), the genomic signature of **asymmetric power** (a conquest-like, stratified outcome), not the balanced admixture that peer collaboration would leave. "Everybody shared the pioneering culture of the times and cooperated" is an appealing narrative that the genetic evidence points *against*.

## Why this matters

**1. It is a clean "color is a smell" catch on our own side.** The cultural-alignment/collaboration story is vivid and flattering to the framework's diffusion instincts — and it is doing too much work. The disciplined framework position needs *no* cultural alignment to explain the outcome: **terrain + wagon mobility (cards 199/204) + the legible pre-pioneered landscape (card 303) already explain the demographic result.** Adding "collaboration among culturally aligned peoples" is a superfluous mechanism that the aDNA actively contradicts. Cut it, or hold it only as "some admixture occurred" (see Calibration 2) — do not let it carry explanatory weight. This is precisely the CLAUDE.md discipline: an anecdote/narrative that feels compelling but doesn't survive the evidence is a warning sign, not a feature.

**2. The framework does not require the expansion to be peaceful — separate mechanism from moral coloring.** Card 281's principle applies directly: mechanistic description is separate from moral evaluation. The terrain-and-technology account predicts the demographic *outcome* (rapid, replacement-dominant expansion) whether the process was collaborative or violent — and the evidence indicates it was closer to overwhelm than to cooperation. The framework's power here is that it *doesn't need the nice story*; it predicts the result from terrain, mobility technology, and landscape legibility alone.

**3. It partially discharges card 303's open prediction.** Card 303 flagged: verify the legibility/fast-exploitation reading against Cunliffe's actual text and the aDNA. Now resolved on both counts. Cunliffe's text: he reads continuity/creolization and cannot isolate a Yamnaya event (because material culture can't) — consistent with the prediction that the population movement would be *hard to see* in his instrument. The aDNA: the movement was real and large — confirming the steppe expansion the occluded-front card is built on. The **speed** mechanism (legibility inheritance) stands; the **collaboration** gloss is removed.

**4. It is a textbook instrument-limitation / two-instruments lesson.** This is the description-vs-explanation and two-instruments theme (cards 266, 273, 276) in its sharpest form: the *same* material record is compatible with continuity *or* replacement; only a second instrument (the genome) disambiguates. Cunliffe's honest continuity reading is what the material instrument alone supports; the framework + aDNA supply the resolution. A model case of "the literature describes what the available instrument can see; the framework and the genome explain what actually happened."

## Calibration

1. **Cunliffe's reading was reasonable for 2008, and this is not a knock on him.** The Anatolian/continuity interpretation was a defensible conclusion from pre-aDNA material culture; several of his cultural-adoption observations remain valid at the margin. Frame this as *the instrument was upgraded*, not *Cunliffe was careless*. (Same generosity as the card 304 framing of his mechanistic agnosticism.)

2. **"Replacement" is dominant but not total — creolization is partly rescued.** The steppe expansion was replacement-*dominant*, not 100% replacement everywhere: **Corded Ware is itself a fusion** of Yamnaya-derived and existing European-farmer ancestry, and admixture proportions vary regionally. So Cunliffe's creolization instinct is not wholly wrong — admixture and cultural fusion did occur. The correction is about *dominant signal and mechanism*: the process was demographic intrusion with asymmetric (male-biased) admixture, not indigenous continuity. Keep "some fusion, regionally variable"; drop "continuity of the same peoples."

3. **Indo-European: steppe, not Anatolian — but hold the strong DOM2-calibrated timing.** The framework sits with the Kurgan/steppe hypothesis (card 189) against Renfrew's Anatolian hypothesis, and aDNA supports the steppe origin for the bulk of the IE expansion into Europe. Note the internal chronology discipline (cards 199/204): the *Yamnaya* expansion (~3300–2500 BC) is wagon-driven; the horse-as-primary-mobility phase is later (Sintashta, post-DOM2 ~2200 BC). Do not let "steppe language spread" collapse into "horseback conquest" for the Yamnaya window specifically.

4. **The male-biased-admixture → asymmetric-power inference is strong but should stay mechanistic, not lurid.** Cards 270/281 read male-biased admixture as the genomic record of asymmetric power; that is the basis for rejecting the peer-collaboration story. State it as a structural/statistical inference about power asymmetry (mechanism), explicitly separated from moral narration (card 281), and avoid over-specifying the lived experience the genome does not record ("the genome records the outcome, not the experience," card 270).

5. **Probabilistic frame.** Terrain + mobility technology + landscape legibility *raised the probability* of a fast, replacement-dominant expansion; regional outcomes varied (more replacement in some zones, more admixture in others). Curve/outcome language stays distributional (cards 010 / 304 / 307).
