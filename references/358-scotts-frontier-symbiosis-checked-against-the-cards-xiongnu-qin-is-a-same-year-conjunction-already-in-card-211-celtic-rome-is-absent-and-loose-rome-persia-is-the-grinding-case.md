## What the project already has

The audit answers Dave's question directly, and the asymmetry is the finding.

- **"Shadow empire" appears in 20 reference cards.** **Xiongnu in 25 cards and 10 chapters.** **"Tribute" in 108 cards.** The steppe-sedentary symbiosis is not a new idea arriving from Scott; it is one of the project's load-bearing mechanisms.
- **Card 211 already does the work Beta proposed as the parallel.** It grounds the mechanism in Thomas Barfield's shadow-empire thesis from *The Perilous Frontier* (1992), dates Maodun's consolidation to ~210 BCE, records the post-Baideng tribute, and disaggregates three sub-modes. Filed 2026-05-14. Beta offered Barfield as the clean test; the project has had it for six months.
- **Barfield is credited by name in exactly one card** while his concept carries twenty. Worth fixing — the project's own sourcing standard says multiple sources per claim and attribution where it is due.
- **The Celtic case is entirely absent.** Zero cards mention oppida, La Tène, the Arverni or the Aedui. If Scott's Celtic claim is load-bearing for him, it is the one half of this the project has never touched.

## The Xiongnu conjunction is tighter than reported

Beta called it "a decade-scale match." It is **the same year.**

- **209 BCE** — Modu Chanyu has his father Touman shot and takes the chanyu-ship.
- **209 BCE** — the Dazexiang uprising under Chen Sheng and Wu Guang, the first large-scale rebellion against Qin.
- 206 BCE — Qin falls.
- 202 BCE — Liu Bang takes the imperial title; Han founded.
- 200 BCE — Battle of Baideng.
- 198 BCE — the heqin settlement: a Han princess to the chanyu, periodic tribute of silk, liquor and rice, equal status between the states, and the Great Wall as mutual border.

Steppe consolidation and sedentary collapse begin in the same year, and the extraction relationship is formalized within eleven. By the standard in card 352 this is a conjunction that *survives* precision rather than dissolving under it, which is the rarer and more valuable outcome. It is also the strongest available support for Scott's symbiosis claim — and it is support the project already held.

## The Celtic case does not tighten, and should be treated accordingly

Beta's chronology, which looks right in outline: La Tène expansion runs from the fifth to fourth centuries BCE, well before Rome's major expansion — the Celts sack Rome around 390 BCE and settle the Po valley. The trade-driven phase is later, second to first century BCE, as Rome pushes into southern Gaul and large tribal polities such as the Arverni and Aedui grow up with the fortified oppida along the wine trade. Diodorus' claim that an amphora of wine bought a slave is probably rhetorical, though the amphora distribution supports brisk trade.

So this is not "rise together." It is a long independent Celtic phase followed by a second, Rome-fed phase, and only the second is symbiosis. **Dave's own question about magnitude settles it:** at decade resolution the Celtic case fails, at century resolution it is a correlation with a four-century head start that the symbiosis mechanism does not explain. Scott's citation is worth checking, because the claim as Dave reports it is doing more work than the chronology supports.

**Recommendation:** treat Xiongnu/Qin as the canonical case and the Celtic material as, at best, a second-phase illustration. Do not let a loose case ride on a tight one's credibility.

## Dave's grinding claim: the project already has the case

Beta softened this, noting that two sedentary states can have a rich border zone and citing Rhine and Danube trade. That softening is aimed at the wrong example — Rome and the Germanic peoples is not two sedentary states.

**The canonical case for Dave's claim is Rome and Sassanid Persia**, and the project already carries it: Sassanid in 9 cards and 5 chapter files, Parthia in 8, Carrhae and the mutual-exhaustion material each in one, and Appendix D holding the Heraclius entries. Centuries of attrition over Mesopotamia and Armenia with little durable territorial change, ending with both powers exhausted and both overrun by the Arab conquest. That is precisely "a grinding of resources," and card 346's backup season already builds a counterfactual on the Khosrow II–Heraclius exhaustion opening the way.

So Dave's intuition is not a new hypothesis needing support. It is a pattern the project has documented without naming as the complement to the shadow-empire mechanism: **a mobile neighbour is extracted from, a sedentary neighbour is ground against.** Worth stating explicitly somewhere, because the two halves together are a stronger claim than either alone.

## Unverified

- Scott's Celtic claim and his citation for it — reported from Dave's reading, not checked against the text.
- The La Tène and oppida chronology is Beta's, confirmed only in outline by Alpha.
- Whether Rome–Persia attrition genuinely lacked a mobile-partner frontier, or whether the Arab and steppe peripheries functioned as one, is not examined here and would complicate the clean contrast.
