# Power-Projection Curve vs. Physical Presence — The Framework's Definition of "Decline" / Projection Peaks and Turns *First*; Presence Lags and Can Still Be Rising / The Gap Between the Two Curves *Is* the Masked Risk

**Source:** Dave Stanton, Beta session 2026-07-09, stated as a standing convention ("pretty much any time I say 'already in decline'…"). Promoted from a clarification inside cards 310/311 to a standalone methodological definition at Dave's go-ahead.
**Type:** foundational methodological definition — disambiguates a core framework term ("decline") and supplies the operational/falsification test for using it
**Relevant to:** every use of "decline / retraction / burnout / overextension" in the dissertation; risk-invisibility-during-optimization (237, "the framework's most uncomfortable prediction"); efficient-but-fragile / safe-failure design (282); succession-inherits-entropy (311) and succession-along-an-energy-gradient (070); empires-as-entropy-engines (038); the Wald-bomber-on-the-record symptom reading (311, 149, 228); the anti-great-man principle

## The distinction

Track two different curves for any polity, and do **not** conflate them:

- **Power-projection capacity** — the ability to *enforce* at a distance: reinforce and relieve peripheral nodes, hold frontiers, compel compliance across the network, absorb a shock at the edge without the center feeling it. This is a *flow / margin* quantity.
- **Physical presence** — population, accumulated wealth, monument-building, territorial extent, apparent prosperity. This is a *stock* quantity.

**The framework's claim: these two curves decouple, and projection peaks and turns down *first*.** Presence lags — it can still be rising (new palaces, peak trade volume, record population) *after* projection has already crested. So when Dave says a civilization is **"already in decline,"** he means **the projection curve is past its maximum and turning down** — the polity is spending an increasing share on frontier defense for diminishing reach — **not** that its physical presence is shrinking. A civilization at its most impressive-looking can already be past peak projection.

## Why this matters

**1. It dissolves a recurring apparent contradiction.** "Already in decline" vs. "prosperous right up to the collapse" are only in tension if "decline" means presence. Under the projection definition they are the **same statement**: presence high, projection turned. The Mycenaean palatial peak (card 311), the Song (237), late imperial Spain (127) all fit — physically impressive, past peak reach.

**2. The gap between the curves is where the risk hides — this is card 237 in curve terms.** Risk-invisibility-during-optimization says an over-optimized system looks healthiest just before it shatters. Restated here: **the vertical distance between still-high presence and already-turned projection is the unpriced risk.** The presence curve is what everyone (including the polity's own elite) reads as "strength"; the projection curve is the one that actually determines whether a shock at the edge cascades to the center. The wider the gap, the more catastrophic and *surprising* the eventual collapse.

**3. It sharpens the Wald-on-the-record / symptom reading (card 311).** A raider or successor arrives *after* projection has turned but *while* presence is still high. The record captures the dramatic agent hitting an apparently strong polity and credits it with the fall — missing that the projection curve had already turned and made the collapse probable. The Sea Peoples, the Comanche filling Mexican weakness (149), the "timing as distraction artifact" — all are shocks landing in the gap between the two curves.

**4. It keeps "decline" falsifiable and non-circular — this is the operational payoff.** The claim "X was already in decline" is **not** supported by pointing at wealth, building programs, or territorial maximum (those are the *presence* curve, which is expected to be high). It is supported only by evidence that the **projection curve** turned: rising frontier-defense share of resources for shrinking reach, loss of ability to reinforce/relieve the periphery, shortening effective reach, shift from offensive/tributary posture to defensive/garrison posture, mercenarization of defense, inability to absorb an edge shock without central disruption. And it is **falsified** by evidence that projection was *still rising* at the moment in question (genuinely expanding, enforceable frontier control — not merely accumulating stock). Any prose that calls a polity "in decline" should be able to point at the projection-curve evidence, not just the presence.

## Calibration

- **Do not "correct" a decline claim by citing prosperity or expansion of presence.** That is a category error (Alpha made it once in the first pass on card 311). Prosperity at the peak is *consistent* with past-peak projection, not evidence against it.
- **When it matters in prose, name the two curves explicitly** — "power-projection decline" vs. "demographic/physical presence" — rather than the bare word "decline," which readers will default-read as presence.
- **This is a lens, not a law.** The two curves *usually* decouple with projection leading; it is an empirical claim to check per case, not an axiom. A polity can also lose presence first (plague, famine) with projection lagging — the reverse decoupling. The framework's frequent case is projection-leads; flag when a case runs the other way.
- Human-factors resonance (Dave's background): this is the gap between a system's *rated performance* and its *remaining margin* — a system running at apparent full performance can have zero margin left. Presence is the performance reading; projection is the margin.
