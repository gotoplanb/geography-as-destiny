# blurb

Geography doesn't determine history. It sets the probability distribution.

Rivers build states. Mountains prevent them. Chokepoints create contests. And every few centuries a technology collapses the friction that made those constraints matter -- the wagon, the ridden horse, the firearm, the railroad, the internet -- and the entire distribution resets.

*Geography as Destiny* argues that framework across eleven chapters, from the Bronze Age steppe to the Nile, the Silk Road, and a pair of parallel conquests that unfolded at the same moment on opposite sides of the planet -- driven by the same technology, against the same kind of culture, with neither side aware of the other. That is not coincidence. That is geography.

Great men, empires, and religions are real. They are outliers on a distribution that terrain drew first. This is not determinism. It is statistics.

# bio

Dave Stanton, PhD, trained as a journalist and mass-communication scholar before a career building software. This book is the framework he spent twenty years accumulating, written in collaboration with Claude. The complete source, reference cards, and revision history are public.
