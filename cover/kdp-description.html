Geography doesn't determine history. It sets the probability distribution.

Rivers build states. Mountains prevent them. Chokepoints create contests. And every few centuries a technology collapses the friction that made those constraints matter — the wagon, the ridden horse, the firearm, the railroad, the internet — and the entire distribution resets.

Geography as Destiny argues that framework across eleven chapters, from the Bronze Age steppe to the Nile, the Silk Road, and a pair of parallel conquests that unfolded at the same moment on opposite sides of the planet — driven by the same technology, against the same kind of culture, with neither side aware of the other. That is not coincidence. That is geography.

Three lenses, every chapter

Geographic determinism. Terrain sets the odds before anyone makes a decision. The thermodynamic logic of river valleys, the economics of position over wealth, the Goldilocks zones where states become possible.

Friction collapse. The book's central original contribution: specific technologies that drop a previously prohibitive cost to near zero and reset which terrain serves whom. The horse on the Great Plains. The railroad across the steppe. Each one redraws the distribution.

Diffusion. Why some innovations race along corridors and others stall at boundaries — and why the boundaries themselves turn out to be load-bearing.

Against the great man

Great men, empires, and religions are real. They are outliers on a distribution that terrain drew first. Genghis Khan appears here as a norm enforcer operating inside constraints, tested against the natural experiment of four successor khanates. The argument respects individuals without centering them.

This is not determinism. It is statistics.

A falsifiable argument

The book names the conditions under which it would be wrong, and tests itself against held-out cases — canals against rivers, corridors that should have developed and didn't. Every claim is sourced to endnotes drawn from 344 reference cards and a working literature review.
